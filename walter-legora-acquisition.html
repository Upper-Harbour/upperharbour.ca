Acquisition Alert Law 25 Impact March 15, 2026

Walter Was Canadian Last Week.
Now It's Swedish.
Your firm may have a new compliance obligation.

By Joshua van Es · Upper Harbour Research

Walter Software Corporation — the Vancouver-based legal AI platform used by Fasken, McCarthy Tétrault, and dozens of other Canadian firms — was acquired by Legora AB of Stockholm on March 12, 2026. Every Canadian law firm using Walter just triggered a new Transfer Impact Assessment requirement under Law 25 that didn't exist last month.

What happened

Walter Software Corporation was incorporated in British Columbia. Its AI platform — used by lawyers for document drafting, editing, blacklining, and matter management — processed privileged legal content through Canadian infrastructure. No Transfer Impact Assessment was required because the data never left Canadian jurisdiction.

On March 12, 2026, Legora AB announced it had acquired Walter. Legora is incorporated in Stockholm, Sweden. The entire Walter team is joining Legora, with the company's core technology being rebuilt into Legora's platform.

The acquisition changes Walter's jurisdictional status from Canadian to Swedish. Under Law 25, any processing of personal information outside Quebec by a foreign-controlled entity requires a documented Transfer Impact Assessment. That obligation didn't exist for Walter users last month. It does now.

The good news

Sweden is not the United States. Legora operates under the EU General Data Protection Regulation — one of the strongest privacy frameworks in the world. There is no Swedish equivalent of the US CLOUD Act. EU law (GDPR Article 48) actively restricts compelled disclosure to foreign governments.

If Walter had been acquired by a US company, the compliance implications would be far more serious. A Swedish/EU parent means the receiving jurisdiction's legal framework provides strong protections — arguably equivalent to or better than Quebec's.

But the TIA requirement is still triggered. Law 25 requires assessment of any transfer outside Quebec, regardless of how protective the receiving jurisdiction is. The assessment may conclude the transfer is acceptable — but the assessment must be documented.

Key Takeaway

Walter under Legora (Sweden/EU) is lower risk than Harvey AI or ChatGPT (US/CLOUD Act). But the TIA requirement is new — firms using Walter need to document the jurisdictional change and assess the transfer. The assessment will likely conclude the transfer is acceptable under GDPR protections.

Why this matters beyond Walter

This acquisition is a textbook example of why ownership monitoring matters for compliance. A tool's jurisdictional status can change overnight — without the client's data physically moving, without any notification to the client, and without any change to the product itself.

Walter is not the first Canadian legal technology company to change hands. Clio acquired vLex. Spellbook is actively acquiring smaller competitors. The legal AI market is consolidating rapidly. Every acquisition potentially changes the compliance status of every client.

Other Recent Ownership Changes

CaseWare — Canadian accounting software — was acquired by Hg Capital (UK private equity). Bench — Canadian bookkeeping — was acquired by Employer.com (US). Wagepoint — Canadian payroll — received majority investment from US private equity. In each case, every Canadian client's compliance status changed. Most clients were never notified.

What law firms should do now

If you use Walter / Legora: Document the ownership change in your compliance records. Conduct a Transfer Impact Assessment for the new Swedish jurisdiction. Confirm with Legora where your data is processed (EU vs. other infrastructure). Execute Legora's GDPR-compliant DPA. The assessment will likely conclude the transfer is acceptable given GDPR protections — but document it.

If you're evaluating legal AI tools: Spellbook (Dialog Enterprises Inc., St. John's NL) is Canadian-parented, CBA exclusive partner, zero data retention, and requires no TIA. For practice management, Clio (Themis Solutions Inc., Toronto) is Canadian-parented. These tools eliminate the TIA requirement entirely.
