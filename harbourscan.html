HarbourScan

Structured jurisdictional compliance assessment for your SaaS stack

HarbourScan maps your organization's SaaS stack to parent jurisdictions, CLOUD Act exposure, and Canadian data residency status — then produces a board-ready compliance gap report. Results are generated locally in your browser and can be saved for your records. Nothing is stored or transmitted by Upper Harbour.

HarbourScan is already being used in early deployments across Canadian regulated sectors.

For: Privacy Officers, General Counsel, CIOs, and procurement leads at organizations with 20+ SaaS tools — particularly those subject to Law 25, operating in regulated sectors, or selling into government.

What HarbourScan produces

Three structured outputs — from initial exposure mapping through to ongoing monitoring. Each draws on the 292-tool Canadian SaaS Sovereignty Index — the same dataset used in HarbourScan deployments for institutional clients.

Core Output
SaaS Sovereignty Audit

A complete jurisdictional map of your organization's SaaS stack. HarbourScan identifies every tool, traces it to its parent company and country of incorporation, assesses CLOUD Act exposure, and produces a compliance gap report with prioritized remediation.

Deliverables
  • Full SaaS stack inventory with jurisdictional mapping
  • CLOUD Act exposure assessment for each tool
  • Canadian data residency status verification
  • DPA and contractual safeguards review
  • Compliance gap report with prioritized risk register
  • Executive summary for board or audit committee
What you'll know
  • Which tools in your stack are CLOUD Act exposed
  • Where data residency claims don't equal sovereignty
  • Which tools require Transfer Impact Assessments
  • Where your DPA coverage has gaps
  • What your overall sovereignty posture looks like
  • What to prioritize first
Compliance Documentation
TIA Documentation Package

Law 25 requires a Transfer Impact Assessment for every cross-border SaaS tool processing personal information of Quebec residents. HarbourScan identifies which tools in your stack require TIAs and produces the jurisdictional analysis and safeguards evaluation needed to complete them.

Deliverables
  • Individual TIA for each cross-border SaaS tool
  • Jurisdictional analysis of parent company legal framework
  • CLOUD Act exposure assessment per tool
  • Contractual safeguards evaluation (DPAs, SCCs)
  • Residual risk determination with mitigation recommendations
  • CAI-ready documentation format
Why this matters now
  • TIAs have been mandatory since September 2023
  • The CAI processed 277 complaints in 2023–2024
  • First post-Law 25 enforcement order issued Sept 2024
  • Penalties up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover
  • Private right of action with $1,000 minimum per violation
  • Class actions are explicitly permitted
Ongoing
Sovereignty Monitoring

Sovereignty isn't static. Vendors get acquired. Corporate structures change. Regulations evolve. New tools enter your stack. The self-assessment provides your baseline. Organizations running HarbourScan operationally receive ongoing tracking of jurisdictional changes across 292 tools — so your compliance posture stays current, not just accurate on the day it was run.

What's included
  • Quarterly sovereignty posture review
  • Acquisition and jurisdiction change alerts
  • New tool assessment as your stack evolves
  • TIA update when vendor circumstances change
  • Regulatory change briefings (Law 25, CPPA, provincial)
  • Annual board-ready sovereignty status report
Why ongoing matters
  • Canadian SaaS companies are acquired regularly — shifting jurisdiction for every client
  • Vendors launch (and retire) Canadian data centre regions
  • The CPPA will change federal requirements when enacted
  • Alberta's PIPA amendments are expected in 2026
  • EU adequacy review of Canada's framework is pending
  • A point-in-time audit goes stale within months
How HarbourScan deploys

Four phases — from initial self-assessment through ongoing monitoring. Each step draws on verified corporate registry data from the Sovereignty Index.

01
Self-assessment
Launch HarbourScan in your browser and begin mapping your SaaS environment against the 292-tool Sovereignty Index database.
02
Stack intake
Select your tools and tag each one with the personal information it processes, the data subjects involved, and the safeguards currently in place.
03
Analysis & documentation
HarbourScan produces jurisdictional assessments, identifies compliance gaps, and generates a prioritized risk register and remediation plan.
04
Delivery & monitoring
Download your board-ready report. The signals pipeline continues to track vendor acquisitions, jurisdiction changes, and regulatory developments affecting your stack.
