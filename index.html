What Upper Harbour produces

Audit-ready documentation aligned to the statutory requirements regulators actually publish. Every assertion traceable to verbatim statute text.

PIA
Privacy Impact Assessments
Full PIAs for federal, BC, Alberta, Ontario, and Quebec regimes. Aligned to OPC, OIPC Alberta, OIPC BC, IPC Ontario, and CAI Quebec templates. Per-data-field operating-program authority mapping.
TIA
Transfer Impact Assessments
Law 25 TIAs grounded in current Quebec Court of Appeal jurisprudence on cross-border transfers and CLOUD Act analysis. Every vendor mapped to ultimate corporate parent, jurisdiction of incorporation, and exposure to foreign legal regimes.
BREACH
Breach assessment packages
72-hour breach assessment workflows, OPC and provincial regulator notification packages, and supporting documentation. Built for regulated industries where timelines and defensibility matter.
PMP
Privacy Management Programs
Privacy management programs and privacy policies aligned to PIPEDA accountability requirements and Law 25 Section 8. Built once, maintained as your stack and operations evolve.

This documentation is now required.

Canadian privacy law has shifted from principles to specific, enforceable documentation obligations. Most organizations don't yet have what they need — and most can't justify the consultant fees to produce it.

Quebec — Law 25
PIAs required for sensitive processing and transfers
Section 3.3 mandates PIAs for personal information transfers outside Quebec. The CAI is enforcing. Penalties up to 4% of worldwide turnover.
Federal — PIPEDA
PMP and accountability documentation
The OPC requires documented privacy management programs as a condition of accountability. Anticipated federal AI legislation will introduce algorithmic impact assessments. Organizations need a defensible record now.
Federal procurement
Buy Canadian + sovereignty disclosures
The Buy Canadian framework and federal sovereignty assessments require vendor jurisdiction disclosure. Canadian software vendors selling to government need to be ready.

The sovereignty stack

Sovereignty operates at every layer of the technology stack. Upper Harbour assesses all of them — but the application layer is where jurisdictional exposure is most acute and most overlooked.

Application layer (SaaS) Payroll, HR, communications, EHR, CRM, analytics — where 82% of tools fall under foreign jurisdiction FOCUS
Platform layer (PaaS) Developer tools, APIs, identity providers, hosted databases
Infrastructure layer (IaaS) Cloud compute, storage, networking — where the policy conversation has focused
Physical layer Data centres, undersea cables, the physical infrastructure of computing
