Why we built this

Privacy is the most concrete expression of data sovereignty Canadians have.

The Canadian sovereignty conversation usually gets stuck on infrastructure — data centres, AI compute, undersea cables. But even when the infrastructure is Canadian, Canadian personal information still moves into foreign jurisdictions one layer up: in the SaaS contracts and processing agreements signed every day.

Privacy compliance is where those choices become visible. Every PIA, every TIA, every breach assessment is a moment to ask the question that matters: whose courts, whose laws, whose subpoenas reach this data? The question is rarely asked. When it is, the answer is buried under legal fees.

Upper Harbour exists to change that — to make Canadian data sovereignty visible, understandable, and actionable, and to make the documentation regulators require something compliance teams can actually produce themselves. The AI privacy agent is one piece. The Sovereignty Index, the methodology, the research that anchors it all — same project: helping Canada see what it has signed up for, and giving organizations the tools to do something about it.

The Sovereignty Index

Three layers deep. Continuously monitored.

Vendors tell you where the data is hosted. The Sovereignty Index tells you whose courts can compel its disclosure, whose subpoenas reach it, and whose terms of service govern it — three layers of legal authority analysis, continuously updated as the SaaS landscape shifts. It’s the data layer underneath every PIA, TIA, and breach assessment the agent produces.

Layer 1
Corporate parent
Every tool traced to its ultimate beneficial owner, jurisdiction of incorporation, and CLOUD Act exposure.
Layer 2
Data transfer
Where data physically moves and is processed. Cross-border transfer routes, regional residency options, and actual processing geographies.
Layer 3
Subprocessor chain
The vendors your vendor relies on, and the jurisdictions they operate under. Most Canadian privacy assessments miss this layer entirely.
Live
Continuously monitored
When a tool’s sovereignty posture changes — M&A, infrastructure shifts, policy revisions — the index updates and you get alerts with the specific lines in your existing documentation that need to change.

This documentation is now required.

Canadian privacy law has shifted from principles to specific, enforceable documentation obligations. Most organizations don’t even know which obligations apply to them — and most who do can’t justify the consultant fees to produce the documentation.

Quebec — Law 25
PIAs required for sensitive processing and transfers
Section 3.3 mandates PIAs for personal information transfers outside Quebec. The CAI is enforcing. Penalties up to 4% of worldwide turnover.
Federal — PIPEDA
PMP and accountability documentation
The OPC requires documented privacy management programs as a condition of accountability. Anticipated federal AI legislation will introduce algorithmic impact assessments. Organizations need a defensible record now.
Federal procurement
Buy Canadian + sovereignty disclosures
The Buy Canadian framework and federal sovereignty assessments require vendor jurisdiction disclosure. Canadian software vendors selling to government need to be ready.
