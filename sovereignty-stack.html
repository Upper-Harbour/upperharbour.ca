The Sovereignty Stack

Canada is securing the bottom of the technology stack. The top — where organizations encounter foreign jurisdiction daily — remains unaddressed.

Scroll to explore↓
The full stack

Digital systems run on four layers.

Hardware at the base. Infrastructure above it. Platforms. And at the top, the SaaS applications employees use every day. Each layer has its own ownership, jurisdiction, and legal exposure.

Where policy focuses

Sovereignty policy targets the bottom two layers.

Sovereign cloud RFI. $2B AI compute. Bell-Hypertec data centres. The March 2026 RFI requires that providers and their ultimate parent corporations are not subject to foreign compelled-disclosure laws. This covers infrastructure and hardware.

Where exposure sits

The top two layers have no equivalent policy.

No federal instrument applies a parent-jurisdiction test to SaaS vendors or the platforms they run on. 82% of the SaaS tools Canadian organizations use are under foreign jurisdiction. 12 entire categories have zero Canadian-owned vendors.

The jurisdiction question

Infrastructure can be domestic. Software can remain governed abroad.

88% of tools advertising Canadian data residency are still under US parent jurisdiction.

Sovereign infrastructure does not automatically produce sovereign systems.
