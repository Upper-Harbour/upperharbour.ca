How each one is doing the work

1. Noir

Charcoal is the stage, oxblood is the spotlight. The radial glow makes the red feel like firelight, not paint. Closest to a Bottega Veneta or private bank energy. Reads as expensive and contemporary at once.

2. Editorial

The 6px oxblood rule on the left edge is the only "color" — the rest is cream and charcoal. Treats oxblood like a publication mark or a serial number. Black square button instead of red button keeps it severe and print-like.

3. Heritage

Oxblood and brass together — the colors of leather-bound legal volumes and brass nameplates. The brass takes over for the eyebrow, link, and CTA outline so oxblood gets to be a background mood rather than every accent. Most "old firm" of the six.

4. Statement

Oxblood is the page, not the accent. Cream type, brass italic accent. This is the only one that says "we are a red brand" instead of "we are a charcoal brand with a red accent." Hardest to pull off, biggest payoff if you commit.

5. Split

60/40 charcoal-to-oxblood vertical split. The hero text lives on charcoal, the right gutter is oxblood. Suggests two sides of a ledger or two columns of a contract. Architectural and a bit aggressive in a good way.

6. Wax Seal

The oxblood is literally a wax seal in the corner — a single contained object on an otherwise restrained charcoal page. The lightest possible touch of oxblood, treated as a symbol of authentication rather than a brand color. Most subtle.
